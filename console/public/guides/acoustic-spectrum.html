Live Microphone Spectrogram & Sound Events

See how a sound changes over time. Fathom plots the microphone spectrum, tracks relative levels and logs likely sound events for inspection.

Start listening

Open the Acoustic feed and choose Enable microphone. The browser asks for microphone permission. Use HTTPS or localhost, and choose the input device in your browser or system settings. The console starts listening only after you enable it.

If permission is denied, enable microphone access for the site in the browser settings and try again. Check that the selected microphone is connected and not muted. No spectrum is displayed as live until microphone input is active.

Read the spectrogram

The spectrogram shows frequency content across time. Brighter areas indicate stronger energy. A steady pitch leaves a narrow band, while short broadband events spread across many frequencies. The level readout uses dBFS, relative to the audio converter’s full scale.

dBFS is not calibrated sound pressure in dB SPL. Microphone gain, distance, hardware and automatic processing all affect the measurement. Use the view to compare events within a session rather than as a calibrated environmental noise meter.

Estimated event classes

The detector opens an event when its level rises 12 dB above the estimated room noise floor and the spectrum changes. Duration, brightness and pitch inform a likely class. These labels are heuristics, not verified identifications.

With suitable stereo input, a timing difference between the channels can estimate left or right bearing. Configure the microphone spacing to match the physical pair. Two microphones cannot resolve front from back, and mono input does not provide stereo direction.

Interface audio and the microphone

Interface cues can enter the microphone through speakers. Fathom briefly suppresses event detection while its own cues play, reducing self-triggered observations. Headphones can further reduce this interference. The Audio control lets you mute cues, change their volume or preview individual sounds.

Custom intro, scanning and warning files are decoded locally for the current session. They replace interface cues; they are not inserted into the microphone signal or presented as detected sound.

Review observations

Choose Sound in the log filters to inspect acoustic events. Export log saves the observations as JSON. The point-cloud recording control records spatial frames, not raw microphone audio. Turn off microphone listening when your session ends.
